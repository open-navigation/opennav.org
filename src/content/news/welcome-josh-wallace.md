**Please Welcome Josh!**

We're thrilled to announce that Josh Wallace has joined Open Navigation full-time as a Nav2 Maintainer! If you've been around the project for the last few years, Josh's name is probably already familiar to you. Odds are you're running his code already right now.

**Not Exactly a New Face**

Josh joins us from Locus Robotics, where he spent years as a Robotics Software Engineer working on navigation for one of the largest fleets of warehouse AMRs in the world. That's thousands of robots driving millions of miles around real warehouses alongside real people, which is about as good a proving ground for navigation software as you can find. He brings a hard-won sense for what actually matters when a robot needs to get from A to B a few hundred thousand times without anyone noticing.

His first Nav2 pull request landed in the Summer of 2021 with the State Lattice Planner. Since then he has opened over 65 pull requests to Nav2, touching just about every corner of the stack. A few highlights you may be using today without knowing who to thank:

If you've used the Smac Planner's State Lattice, you've used Josh's work. He worked alongside Matt Booker and Steve Macenski to bring it to life and is a co-author on the [Smac Planner paper](/news/smac-planner-paper), now published in IEEE Robotics and Automation Practice.

Remember when we bragged that every Nav2 action now returns contextual error codes for intelligent behavior planning? That was Josh too. He piped error codes through the planner, controller, smoother, and behavior servers, then wired them into the Behavior Tree.

He was also there from the very beginning of the Route Server, writing the graph file parser, example graphs, and the tooling to create them.

**Nav2 & You**

So what does having Josh on board full-time mean for you?

**Faster response times** on issues, PRs, and reviews, for one. Our community has grown enormously and another experienced set of eyes on the project goes a long way.

It also means **more capabilities, faster**, especially in planning, route-based navigation, and performance optimization for the compute that robots actually ship with.

And it means more **resilience** for the project. Nav2 has been through three host organizations losing interest or shutting down. Adding another person deeply familiar with the entire stack, full-time, is another step toward the long-term stability that our users stake their businesses on.

None of this is possible without our sponsors. Their support is what lets Open Navigation bring on people like Josh, and this is exactly what that support is for. Thank you to Dexory, NVIDIA, Polymath Robotics, AMD, Intel, Stereolabs, Staer AI, and all of our Friends of Nav2 sponsors for making it happen. If your company relies on Nav2, please consider joining them so we can keep growing (*cough*, we're always happy to welcome more Joshes).

Please give Josh a warm welcome when you see him on GitHub, Discourse, or at the next ROSCon!
